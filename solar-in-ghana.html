Solar panels in Ghana

Ghana has excellent sun and a serious reliability problem — the famous “dumsor” power cuts — which makes solar-plus-battery not a luxury but a necessity for homes and businesses. This guide covers sun hours, tariffs, the reliability argument, and realistic costs.

☀️ Peak sun hours in Ghana by region

Peak sun hours (PSH) is the number of hours per day the sun delivers 1000 W/m². Ghana sits near the equator with excellent irradiation: about 5.0–5.5 PSH on average — Accra and the coast are around 5.0–5.2, the northern savannah (Tamale, Wa) climbs toward 5.4–5.6. The Harmattan dust season (December–February) lowers output somewhat — a wash-down routine keeps panels near spec. See our guide on peak sun hours by location.

⚡ Electricity prices and reliability in Ghana

Grid electricity costs roughly GH₵1.0–1.8/kWh all-in depending on the block — and the deeper cost is unreliability. Scheduled and unscheduled outages (“dumsor”) still hit many areas several times a week, so homes and businesses already spend heavily on petrol generators: fuel alone commonly costs the equivalent of GH₵300–800+ per month for a shop. Solar with battery directly replaces that spend. A hybrid setup keeps the grid connection for cheap baseline power and lets the battery + solar carry the outages. Estimate your saving with the solar bill savings calculator.

💡 Why solar-plus-battery is the smartest move in Ghana

Because the alternative is a generator. A 2 kVA petrol generator running a shop's fridges and lights for 6 evening hours burns fuel worth several dollars a day, needs constant maintenance, and dies when fuel prices spike. A solar + lithium system of the same output costs a few months' worth of that fuel, then runs free for 10–15 years (battery life) with panels lasting 25+. Payback against generator replacement is commonly 1.5–3 years. Off-grid and mini-grid deployments are also growing fast in rural Ghana where the grid never arrives — read our grid-tied vs off-grid vs hybrid guide.

🛠️ Field note

In markets like Ghana's, the right question is never “is solar cheaper than grid power?” — it's “is solar cheaper than the generator I already own?” The generator maths almost always loses: fuel, oil, plugs, and downtime during the exact hours a shop needs refrigeration. Two field tips: use LiFePO4 batteries, not lead-acid — the price gap has closed and LiFePO4 survives 3,000+ cycles in hot climates; and plan the panel wash schedule around Harmattan, because dust buildup quietly cuts 15–25% of production.

❓ Frequently asked questions

Is solar worth it in Ghana?

Yes — often the fastest payback of any market: against petrol-generator fuel (GH₵300–800+/month), a solar+battery system commonly pays back in 1.5–3 years.

How many sun hours does Ghana get?

Excellent — about 5.0–5.5 PSH on average. The north (Tamale) is highest at ~5.4–5.6; Accra and the coast ~5.0–5.2. Harmattan dust (Dec–Feb) reduces output; wash panels regularly.

Should I go fully off-grid in Ghana?

Usually hybrid is smarter: stay connected to the grid for cheap baseline power, and let solar + battery cover outages and peak hours. Full off-grid only where the grid is absent or unusable.

How much does a solar system with battery cost in Ghana?

Roughly GH₵6,000–9,000 for a 1.5 kW + 2.4 kWh shop/home starter system, GH₵12,000–17,000 for a 3 kW family system. Prices move — get local quotes.

Note: tariffs, net-billing rules and prices in Ghana change over time. The figures here are approximate, for planning only — always verify with your utility and local installers.
