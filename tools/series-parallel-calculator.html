Once you know how many panels you want, the next decision is how to wire them. The same set of panels can be arranged as one long series string, several parallel strings, or a mix — and while the total power never changes, the voltage and current the array delivers change a lot. Getting that mix right is what keeps the array inside your inverter or charge controller's safe window.

Series adds voltage, parallel adds current

The two rules are simple. Wire panels in series (end to end) and their voltages add up while the current stays the same as one panel. Wire strings in parallel (side by side) and their currents add up while the voltage stays the same as one string. So a layout of S panels in series and P parallel strings gives an array voltage of Vmp × S and an array current of Imp × P.

Power is the same either way

A common misconception is that series or parallel gives you "more power." It doesn't. Total power is just the number of panels times their wattage, no matter how you wire them. What the wiring changes is the delivery: series gives high voltage and low current (thinner cable, less voltage drop over distance), parallel gives low voltage and high current. You pick the balance that fits your equipment.

Open-circuit voltage is the safety limit

The number that really matters for safety is the open-circuit voltage (Voc) — the highest voltage the string can reach, with no load, in cold weather. It's higher than the operating Vmp, and it rises with every panel you add in series and climbs further as temperature drops. Your string's cold Voc must stay below the maximum input voltage of your inverter or charge controller, or you risk damaging it. That's the real ceiling on how many panels go in one series string. Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate solar panels wired in series and parallel?

Series adds voltage (Vmp × panels in series), parallel adds current (Imp × strings in parallel), and total power is Vmp × Imp × series × parallel. This calculator returns all four from your spec-sheet values.

Does wiring panels in series or parallel change the total power?

No. Total power depends only on the number of panels and their wattage. Series gives higher voltage at lower current; parallel gives lower voltage at higher current — same power, different delivery.

Why does open-circuit voltage (Voc) matter for series strings?

Voc is the string's highest voltage, reached cold with no load. It rises with each series panel and as temperature drops, and must stay below your inverter or controller's maximum input voltage — so it limits panels per string.
