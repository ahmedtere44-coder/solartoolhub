Watts to amps calculator

Watts measure power; amps measure current. To go from one to the other you need the voltage, because power is voltage times current — written P = V × I. Rearranged for current, that's simply amps = watts ÷ volts. It's one of the most useful conversions in any electrical job, because fuses, breakers, and cables are all rated in amps, not watts.

A worked example

Say you have a 1000 W load on a 220 V supply. The current is 1000 ÷ 220 ≈ 4.5 A. Put the same 1000 W load on a 12 V system and the current jumps to 1000 ÷ 12 ≈ 83 A. Same power, but nearly twenty times the current — which is exactly why low-voltage DC runs need much thicker cable and bigger fuses.

DC vs AC

For DC and simple resistive loads, amps = watts ÷ volts is exact. For AC loads with a power factor (motors, some electronics), you divide by the power factor too: amps = watts ÷ (volts × power factor). Most solar DC-side calculations use the simple form.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert watts to amps?

Divide watts by volts: amps = watts ÷ volts, from P = V × I. For AC with a power factor, divide by the power factor too.

How many amps is 1000 watts?

About 4.5 A at 220 V, 8.3 A at 120 V, or 83 A at 12 V. Lower voltage means more current for the same power.

Is the watts to amps formula different for DC and AC?

For DC and resistive loads, amps = watts ÷ volts. For reactive AC loads, divide by the power factor as well.
