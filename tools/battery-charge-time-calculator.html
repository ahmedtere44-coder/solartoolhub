Whether you're sizing a charge controller or just wondering when your bank will be ready, charge time comes down to a simple relationship between capacity and current — with one real-world adjustment for the energy lost as heat while charging. This calculator handles both, and tells you the C-rate you're charging at so you can check it's safe for your battery.

The basic formula

At its simplest:

Charge time (hrs) = capacity (Ah) ÷ charging current (A)

A 100 Ah battery charged at 20 A takes about 100 ÷ 20 = 5 hours in theory. But charging isn't perfectly efficient — some energy is lost as heat — so the realistic time is a bit longer. Dividing by the charging efficiency (say 85%) gives about 5.9 hours. This calculator shows both the ideal and the realistic figure.

What is the C-rate?

The C-rate is the charging current expressed relative to capacity. Charging a 100 Ah battery at 100 A is 1C (about an hour), at 50 A is 0.5C (about two hours), and at 20 A is 0.2C (about five hours). It's a quick way to check whether your charging current is reasonable — and crucially, whether it's within what your battery can safely accept.

How fast is too fast?

Every battery has a maximum safe charge rate. Lead-acid batteries usually shouldn't be charged much above 0.2–0.3C, or they overheat, gas, and wear out faster. Lithium batteries typically accept 0.5C or more, but still have a limit. Charging faster than recommended shortens battery life, so it's worth staying within the manufacturer's figure even though it means waiting longer. The calculator flags the C-rate so you can see where you stand.

Charging from partly full

You rarely charge from completely empty. If your battery is already 50% full, you only need to replace the other 50%, which halves the time. The "charge needed (%)" input handles this — set it to how much you're actually topping up.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to charge a solar battery?

Divide capacity (Ah) by charging current (A) for the basic hours, then allow for losses. A 100 Ah battery at 20 A takes ~5 hours ideally, or ~5.5–6 hours realistically. Charging from partly full is proportionally faster.

What is the C-rate of a battery?

Charge/discharge current relative to capacity. 1C fills in ~1 hour, 0.5C in ~2, 0.2C in ~5. For a 100 Ah battery, 0.2C = 20 A. Lead-acid often maxes at 0.2–0.3C; lithium accepts more.

Can I charge a battery too fast?

Yes — charging above the recommended C-rate makes heat and shortens life (and for lead-acid causes gassing). Staying within the rated C-rate keeps the battery healthy.
