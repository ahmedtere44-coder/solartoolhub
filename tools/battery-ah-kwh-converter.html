Batteries are rated in amp-hours, but everything else in a solar system — your daily use, your panel output, your bill — is in kilowatt-hours. Converting between them is simple once you bring voltage into the picture, and it's the step that lets you compare a battery to your actual energy needs.

The conversion

Amp-hours only tell you half the story; you also need the voltage. Multiply them to get watt-hours, then divide by 1000 for kilowatt-hours: kWh = Ah × V ÷ 1000. A 200 Ah battery at 12 V holds 200 × 12 = 2400 Wh, or 2.4 kWh. The same 200 Ah at 48 V holds four times as much — 9.6 kWh — which is exactly why larger systems use higher battery voltages.

Why usable capacity is less than the label

You can't safely use all of a battery's rated capacity. Depth of discharge (DoD) is the fraction you can draw before recharging: around 50% for lead-acid and 80–90% for lithium. Draining deeper than that repeatedly shortens the battery's life. So usable energy is the total times the DoD — a 2.4 kWh battery at 80% DoD gives about 1.9 kWh you can actually count on.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert amp hours (Ah) to kilowatt hours (kWh)?

Multiply amp-hours by voltage for watt-hours, then divide by 1000: kWh = Ah × V ÷ 1000. A 200 Ah, 12 V battery is 2400 Wh = 2.4 kWh.

What is usable battery capacity and depth of discharge?

Depth of discharge is the share you can safely use — ~50% for lead-acid, 80–90% for lithium. Usable capacity is total capacity × DoD; going deeper shortens battery life.

How many kWh is a 200Ah 12V battery?

2.4 kWh total (200 × 12 ÷ 1000). At 80% depth of discharge, about 1.9 kWh is usable.
