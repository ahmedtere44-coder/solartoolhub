kVA to kW calculator

Convert apparent power to real power in one step. Enter the kVA and the power factor to get kW — the number that matters for sizing generators, inverters, and transformers. Based on kW = kVA × power factor.

kW = kVA × power factor. The reactive part is kVAR = kVA × sin(arccos PF). Together they form the power triangle: kVA² = kW² + kVAR². At a power factor of 1, kVA and kW are equal.

For a fixed kVA, kW rises with power factor.

A generator or inverter is rated in kVA — apparent power. But the load it actually drives is measured in kW — real power. The two are not the same, and the gap between them is set by the power factor. The conversion is simply kW = kVA × power factor. It's one of the first sums you do when matching a generator to a load.

A worked example

Take a 10 kVA generator rated at the usual 0.8 power factor. Its real output is 10 × 0.8 = 8 kW. The reactive part is 10 × sin(arccos 0.8) = 6 kVAR. Notice the neat 3-4-5 shape: 8 kW, 6 kVAR, and 10 kVA form a right triangle, because kVA² = kW² + kVAR². That triangle is the whole story of apparent, real, and reactive power in one picture.

Why it matters

If you size a generator by its kVA number and ignore the power factor, you'll overestimate how much real load it can carry. A 10 kVA set won't run 10 kW of equipment — only 8 kW at a 0.8 power factor. For purely resistive loads like heaters, the power factor is close to 1, so kVA and kW nearly match; for motors it's lower, so the shortfall is bigger.

Frequently asked questions

How do you convert kVA to kW?

Multiply kVA by the power factor: kW = kVA × PF. For example, 10 kVA at 0.8 is 8 kW. To reverse it, divide: kVA = kW ÷ PF.

What is a typical power factor?

Generators are usually rated at 0.8. Resistive loads like heaters sit near 1.0; motors and mixed loads fall between 0.7 and 0.85.

What is the difference between kVA and kW?

kVA is apparent power the source must supply; kW is the real power that does work. The power factor links them, and the difference shows up as reactive power in kVAR.
