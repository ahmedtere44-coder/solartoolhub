Solar panels in Indonesia

Indonesia is a vast tropical archipelago with strong, steady sun and a huge, growing electricity demand. Rooftop solar is expanding, though the rules around exporting to the PLN grid have shifted, so self-consumption is often the focus.

☀️ Peak sun hours in Indonesia

Indonesia's equatorial climate gives steady sun year-round — roughly 4.5 to 5.5 peak sun hours, with drier eastern islands higher and the wettest regions a little lower.

RegionApprox. peak sun hours (annual avg)
Nusa Tenggara & the east~5.2 – 5.7
Java & Bali~4.7 – 5.3
Sulawesi~4.7 – 5.3
Sumatra~4.4 – 5.0
Kalimantan (wettest)~4.2 – 4.8

⚡ Electricity prices in Indonesia

Grid electricity from PLN is billed by customer class, commonly around the equivalent of $0.10–$0.11 per kWh for many residential tariffs. Because power is relatively affordable, the strongest solar case is for higher-consumption homes and businesses that self-consume during the day.

💸 Incentives & net metering

Indonesia has a rooftop-solar regulation administered by PLN, but the export/credit terms have changed over time and at points limited or removed net-export credit, pushing systems toward self-consumption. Rules continue to evolve, so confirm the latest PLN rooftop policy before sizing anything that relies on exporting surplus.

💡 Local insight: Indonesia

Indonesia's sun is steady and abundant, but the economics hinge on policy: with affordable PLN tariffs and export rules that have tightened, the best returns come from sizing a system to match your daytime use rather than to export surplus. For the thousands of islands without reliable grid access, off-grid solar-plus-battery is often the practical way to get power at all.

❓ Frequently asked questions

How many peak sun hours does Indonesia get?

About 4.5 to 5.5, steady year-round due to its equatorial climate. The drier eastern islands are higher; the wettest regions like Kalimantan a little lower.

Is solar worth it in Indonesia?

For higher-consumption homes and businesses, yes — sized for daytime self-consumption. Affordable PLN tariffs and changing export rules mean self-consumption usually beats relying on export credit.

Does Indonesia have net metering?

There is a PLN rooftop regulation, but export/credit terms have changed over time and at points been limited, favouring self-consumption. Confirm the latest PLN policy before installing.

How much does solar cost in Indonesia?

Roughly $3,500–$5,800 for a 5 kWp home system. Get local quotes.

Note: Indonesian PLN tariffs, rooftop-export rules and prices change over time. These figures are approximate, for planning only — verify with PLN and a local installer.
