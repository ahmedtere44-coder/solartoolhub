Solar panels in Ethiopia

Ethiopia combines some of the best solar resource in Africa with a grid that barely reaches rural areas — making solar not just attractive but often the only practical power source. This guide covers sun hours, the energy landscape, costs and what to buy.

☀️ Peak sun hours in Ethiopia by region

Peak sun hours (PSH) is the number of hours per day the sun delivers 1000 W/m². Ethiopia's highlands sit almost directly under the sun belt with some of the best resource in Africa: about 5.5–6.2 PSH — among the highest on earth. The western lowlands (Gambella) and the east (Harar, Dire Dawa) are sunniest; Addis Ababa at 2,400 m elevation averages ~5.5–5.8. Altitude actually helps — thinner atmosphere means more direct irradiance. See our guide on peak sun hours by location.

⚡ Electricity prices and the grid reality in Ethiopia

Ethiopian Electric Power runs one of the cheapest tariffs in the world where the grid exists — roughly ~ETB 3–10/kWh (~€0.02–0.05) — but coverage is the real story: only about a third of rural households have any grid connection at all, and urban supply is unreliable with frequent cuts. For the majority of the country, the practical choice is not “solar vs grid” but “solar vs kerosene, candles and nothing”. Off-grid solar home systems and solar water pumps are transforming rural farming and household life, while urban homes and businesses increasingly add solar to survive outages. 💡 Where solar wins in Ethiopia

Three clear segments: rural off-grid homes, where a solar home system (lighting + phone charging + small TV) replaces kerosene at a payback of well under a year; farming and water pumping, where solar pumps replace diesel on the vast majority of farms with no grid access — see our solar water pump calculator; and urban backup, where businesses losing hours of supply daily install solar + battery to keep fridges, POS and lighting alive. In all three, the comparator is not the cheap grid tariff — it's diesel, kerosene, or no power at all.

🛠️ Field note

Ethiopia teaches the most important lesson in solar: the grid tariff is often the wrong benchmark. For most of the country, solar competes against kerosene lamps, phone-charging fees and diesel pumps — and beats all of them easily. Two practical notes from the ground: buy from established suppliers with real warranties — the market has cheap imports that die in one season — and for water pumping, size the pump around the dry-season water table, not the rainy season, because that is when the crop needs it most and when the sun is actually strongest.

❓ Frequently asked questions

Is solar worth it in Ethiopia?

Yes — for most of the country it is the only practical power source. Off-grid systems replace kerosene and diesel with payback often under 1–3 years.

How many sun hours does Ethiopia get?

Some of the best in Africa — about 5.5–6.2 PSH. The eastern lowlands are highest; Addis Ababa ~5.5–5.8. High altitude increases direct irradiance.

What size solar system does a rural home need?

A basic solar home system (100–300 W, ~US$80–250) covers lighting, phone charging and a small TV. Add a battery (~100–200 Ah LiFePO4) for evening use.

Can solar run a water pump for a farm in Ethiopia?

Yes — solar water pumps are the standard solution where the grid is absent. Use the solar water pump calculator to size flow, head and array for your well.
