Solar panels in the Netherlands

The Netherlands surprises most people: it is far from the sunniest place in Europe, yet it has more solar per person than almost anywhere on earth. High electricity prices, simple installations and historically strong net metering make rooftop solar one of the best investments a Dutch household can make. This guide covers sun hours, tariffs, the phase-out of the old scheme and realistic costs and payback.

☀️ Peak sun hours in The Netherlands by region

Peak sun hours (PSH) is the number of hours per day the sun delivers 1000 W/m². The Netherlands averages only about 2.9–3.1 PSH — far less than Jordan or Spain — but cool temperatures make panels a few percent more efficient, and long summer days (16+ hours of daylight) compensate for cloudy winters. ⚡ Electricity price and net metering in the Netherlands

The Netherlands historically used salderingsregeling (net metering): every kWh you exported was credited 1:1 against consumption, so a small system could zero the bill. From 2025 the scheme is being phased out gradually, and from 2031 export is compensated at a lower, market-based rate instead. Meanwhile, household electricity prices are high — roughly €0.35–0.45 per kWh all-in — so even with lower export value, self-consumption solar still saves a lot. A battery is becoming attractive precisely because exported kWh are worth less than consumed ones. Estimate your saving with the solar bill savings calculator.

💡 Why solar is still booming despite low sun hours

High retail electricity prices are the real driver: every kWh you generate and use yourself saves €0.35–0.45, which is 3–4× more than the value of sun hours alone would suggest. Rooftop PV in the Netherlands is simple — mostly small residential systems, no permit needed below 1 MW on roofs — and installation culture is mature, so prices are low and payback is fast. Read our grid-tied vs off-grid vs hybrid guide — the Dutch answer is grid-tied, increasingly with battery.

🛠️ Field note

The Dutch case shows that sun hours are only half the story. The Netherlands has modest sun, yet huge solar adoption — because prices are high, installations are simple and roofs are abundant. When you evaluate solar anywhere, weigh three things together: sun hours, the retail price of the electricity you offset, and the export/net-metering rules. In a cloudy country with expensive power, solar can still pay back faster than in a sunny country with cheap power.

❓ Frequently asked questions

Is solar worth it in the Netherlands with so little sun?

Yes — it is one of Europe's biggest solar markets. Low sun is offset by high electricity prices (€0.35–0.45/kWh), low installation costs and historically 1:1 net metering, giving typical payback of 4–7 years.

How does net metering (saldering) work there?

The Dutch salderingsregeling credited exported kWh 1:1 against consumption. It is being phased out from 2025 and ends in 2031, replaced by lower market-based export compensation — so self-consumption and batteries grow in importance.

Do I need a permit for rooftop solar in the Netherlands?

Generally no — roof-mounted systems under 1 MW do not require a permit. Ground-mounted and larger systems need one. Always confirm with your municipality for local heritage or zoning rules.

How much does a 5kW home system cost in the Netherlands?

Roughly €5,500–7,500 installed for a 5 kW system. Prices vary with panel quality, roof type and installer — always get at least three local quotes.

Note: tariffs, net-billing rules and prices in The Netherlands change over time. The figures here are approximate, for planning only — always verify with your utility and local installers.
