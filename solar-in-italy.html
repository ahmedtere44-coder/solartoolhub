Solar panels in Italy

Italy combines good Mediterranean sun with some of Europe's higher electricity prices, so rooftop solar is well worth it — especially in the south. Incentives and self-consumption schemes further improve the payback.

☀️ Peak sun hours in Italy

Italy has a clear north-south sun split — roughly 3.5 to 5 peak sun hours. Sicily and the south are highest; the northern plains lower.

RegionApprox. peak sun hours (annual avg)
Sicily & the far south~4.6 – 5.0
Southern mainland (Puglia, Calabria)~4.3 – 4.8
Central Italy (Rome, Tuscany)~4.0 – 4.5
Po Valley & the north~3.5 – 4.1
Alpine north~3.3 – 3.9

⚡ Electricity prices in Italy

Italian households pay high electricity prices — often around €0.25–€0.35 per kWh. That high grid cost is the main driver of solar savings: every kWh you self-generate is worth a lot, so even the cloudier north can make solar pay.

💸 Incentives & net metering

Italy supports rooftop solar through self-consumption plus surplus payments (scambio sul posto historically, evolving toward newer rules), tax-deduction schemes (detrazioni) that spread part of the cost over several years, and support for shared "energy communities". Rules change often, so check the current national deductions and your region's programmes.

💡 Local insight: Italy

As with Spain, the return in Italy is best when you self-consume your own power, since surplus is paid at a lower rate than grid electricity. The south generates far more than the north for the same system, so paybacks in Sicily and Puglia (often 6–9 years) beat the northern plains. Tax deductions can materially shorten the effective payback if you can use them.

❓ Frequently asked questions

How many peak sun hours does Italy get?

About 3.5 to 5. Sicily and the south are highest (~4.6–5), central Italy around 4–4.5, and the northern Po Valley lower at ~3.5–4.1.

Is solar worth it in Italy?

Yes — high electricity prices plus incentives give a typical payback of about 6–9 years, faster in the south. Self-consumption and tax deductions improve the return.

What incentives does Italy offer for solar?

Self-consumption with surplus payments, tax-deduction schemes (detrazioni) spread over several years, and support for energy communities. Rules change, so check the current national and regional programmes.

How much do solar panels cost in Italy?

Roughly €1,300–€1,900 per kWp, so about €4,500–€6,500 for a 3 kWp system. Get local quotes.

Note: Italian electricity prices, incentive/tax-deduction rules and prices change over time. These figures are approximate, for planning only — verify locally.
