Solar panels in Costa Rica

Costa Rica generates almost all its electricity from renewables — yet rooftop solar is spreading fast, because retail tariffs are moderate-to-high and self-consumption cuts the bill directly. This guide covers sun hours, tariffs, net billing, and realistic costs and payback.

☀️ Peak sun hours in Costa Rica by region

Peak sun hours (PSH) is the number of hours per day the sun delivers 1000 W/m². Costa Rica averages about 5.0–5.4 PSH — the Pacific side (Guanacaste, Puntarenas) is sunniest with a long dry season, while the Caribbean side (Limón) is cloudier with more rain days. The dry season (December–April) is when systems truly earn their keep. See our guide on peak sun hours by location.

⚡ Electricity tariff and net billing in Costa Rica

The ICE grid charges residential customers roughly ₵75–95 per kWh (~US$0.14–0.17) plus fixed charges, with a progressive structure — higher usage blocks pay more. Costa Rica operates net billing: exported energy is credited against your consumption within the billing period at the regulated rate, which keeps the economics of export healthy (unlike markets that pay rock-bottom export rates). Air conditioning and hot water dominate the bill in the Central Valley's mild-but-humid climate, and both align reasonably well with daytime solar production. Estimate your saving with the solar bill savings calculator.

💡 Why solar is growing despite a green grid

Because the grid being green doesn't make your bill smaller. At ₵75–95/kWh, every self-consumed solar kWh saves real money, and net billing keeps export value fair, so payback for a well-sized rooftop system lands around 5–8 years. For tourism businesses — hotels, restaurants, surf shops — solar also signals sustainability to guests, which in Costa Rica's eco-tourism economy is a real revenue factor. Read our grid-tied vs off-grid vs hybrid guide — in Costa Rica the answer is grid-tied with net billing.

🛠️ Field note

Costa Rica's interesting dynamic: a nearly 100% renewable grid, yet rooftop solar keeps growing — proof that people adopt solar for the bill, not the grid's colour. Two practical notes: the dry season (Dec–Apr) on the Pacific side is when systems over-produce relative to the wet season, so with net billing you effectively bank those months; and humidity is the silent killer — use corrosion-rated connectors and frames near the coasts, because salt air eats cheap hardware within two seasons.

❓ Frequently asked questions

Is solar worth it in Costa Rica?

Yes — at ₵75–95/kWh with fair net billing, a well-sized rooftop system typically pays back in 5–8 years.

How many sun hours does Costa Rica get?

About 5.0–5.4 PSH — the Pacific side (Guanacaste) is sunniest (~5.3–5.5) with a long dry season; the Caribbean side (Limón) is cloudier (~4.4–4.7).

How does net billing work in Costa Rica?

Exported energy is credited against your consumption within the billing period at the regulated tariff rate — unlike markets that pay rock-bottom export prices, Costa Rica keeps export value fair.

How much does a 5kW system cost in Costa Rica?

Roughly US$6,000–8,500 installed. Prices vary by region and roof — get local quotes and confirm the installer is certified for grid interconnection.

Note: tariffs, net-billing rules and prices in Costa Rica change over time. The figures here are approximate, for planning only — always verify with your utility and local installers.
