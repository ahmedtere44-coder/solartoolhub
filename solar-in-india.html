Solar panels in India

India is one of the world's biggest and fastest-growing solar markets. Strong sunshine, a national rooftop subsidy (PM Surya Ghar) and net metering in most states make residential solar a smart long-term investment.

☀️ Peak sun hours in India

India gets strong sun almost everywhere — roughly 4.5 to 6 peak sun hours. The north-west (Rajasthan, Gujarat) is highest; the monsoon-heavy north-east a little lower.

RegionApprox. peak sun hours (annual avg)
Rajasthan & Gujarat~5.5 – 6.0
Central India & the Deccan~5.2 – 5.7
South India (TN, Karnataka, AP)~5.0 – 5.6
North India (Delhi, UP, Punjab)~4.8 – 5.4
North-east & heavy-monsoon belt~4.0 – 4.6

Use your local figure in the solar panel calculator and the energy production calculator. Background: peak sun hours by location.

⚡ Electricity prices in India

Residential electricity in India is billed on rising slabs and varies a lot by state, but is roughly ₹6–₹10 per kWh for typical households, and higher for large consumers. The more you use, the higher the slab — so solar is most valuable for homes with big monthly bills.

💸 Incentives & net metering

The big one is PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana, a central subsidy for residential rooftop solar (for example, up to around ₹78,000 for a 3 kW system, subject to current rules). Most states also offer net metering, crediting exported energy against your bill. Subsidy amounts and net-metering caps change, so check the latest with your DISCOM and the national portal.

💰 System cost & payback in India

Before subsidy, rooftop systems run roughly ₹50,000–₹70,000 per kW; the PM Surya Ghar subsidy brings the net cost down significantly:

System sizeApprox. costSuits
3 kW~₹1.5–2.1 lakh before subsidyTypical home
5 kW~₹2.5–3.5 lakh before subsidyLarger home / higher bill
10 kW~₹5–7 lakh before subsidyLarge home / small business

💡 Local insight: India

India's combination of a national subsidy plus net metering makes residential rooftop solar pay back quickly — often in about 4–6 years for homes in higher tariff slabs. The key is to size the system to your actual consumption and to confirm your state's net-metering cap, since some states limit the system size eligible for full net metering.

❓ Frequently asked questions

How many peak sun hours does India get?

About 4.5 to 6. Rajasthan and Gujarat are highest (~5.5–6), most of the south and centre around 5–5.7, and the monsoon-heavy north-east lower at ~4–4.6.

Is solar worth it in India?

Yes for most homes, especially with the PM Surya Ghar subsidy and net metering. Payback is commonly around 4–6 years for higher-consumption households.

What is the PM Surya Ghar subsidy?

A central-government scheme that subsidises residential rooftop solar — for example up to roughly ₹78,000 for a 3 kW system, subject to the current rules on the national portal.

How much does solar cost in India?

Roughly ₹50,000–₹70,000 per kW before subsidy, so a 3 kW system is about ₹1.5–2.1 lakh before the PM Surya Ghar subsidy reduces it.

Note: Indian electricity tariffs, the PM Surya Ghar subsidy and state net-metering rules change over time and vary by state/DISCOM. These figures are approximate, for planning only — verify locally.
