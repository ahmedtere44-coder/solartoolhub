Solar panels in Colombia

High-voltage cities, net billing and zero VAT on solar equipment — fast-growing rooftops from Bogotá to the Caribbean coast.

Egypt gets outstanding sun — roughly 5.5 to 6.5 peak sun hours. Upper Egypt and the desert are highest; the Delta and north coast a little lower.

⚡ Electricity prices in Colombia

Household electricity runs roughly COP 800–1,100 per kWh depending on your utility and stratum — among the higher tariffs in South America, and rising.

💸 Incentives & net metering

Colombia's net billing scheme (Law 1715) credits exports against bills, and solar equipment carries zero VAT (IVA) with accelerated depreciation for businesses — a rare double incentive in Latin America.

💡 Local insight: Colombia

Bogotá's altitude is the hidden advantage: panels run cooler at 2,600 m, so real output beats the paper rating — but hail in the highlands destroys thin-glass modules, so specify 3.2 mm tempered glass rated for hail class 3. The zero-VAT rule applies only when the equipment invoice is properly classified under the solar exemption code; many owners lose the benefit to a paperwork slip.

❓ Frequently asked questions

How many peak sun hours does Colombia get?

Roughly 4.0 to 5.8. The Caribbean coast is superb (~5.3–5.8), Bogotá sits around 4.0–4.4 thanks to altitude cloud, and the Llanos reach 4.6–5.0.

Is solar worth it in Colombia?

Yes — rising tariffs, zero VAT on equipment and net billing under Law 1715 bring payback to roughly 5–8 years.

Does Colombia have net metering?

Colombia uses net billing (Law 1715): exported power is credited against future bills at regulated prices.

How much does solar cost in Colombia?

Roughly COP 2.6–3.6 million per kW installed for a typical home system.

Note: Colombia electricity tariffs, net-metering rules and prices change frequently (and with the currency). These figures are approximate, for planning only — verify with your utility and a local installer.
