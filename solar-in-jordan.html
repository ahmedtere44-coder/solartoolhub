Solar panels in Jordan

Jordan is one of the sunniest countries on earth, and with net metering in place, rooftop solar is one of the best energy investments a household or business can make here. This guide covers sun hours by region, the electricity tariff blocks, net metering, and realistic costs and payback.

☀️ Peak sun hours in Jordan by region

Peak sun hours (PSH) is the number of hours per day the sun delivers 1000 W/m² — it's the single most important number for sizing a solar system. Jordan averages roughly 5.5–6 PSH across the year, which is excellent. The desert south is highest; the northern highlands are a little lower.

⚡ Electricity tariff in Jordan (residential)

Jordan uses a block (tiered) tariff: the more you use, the higher the rate on the extra units. There's a subsidised tariff for eligible households (registered on the support platform) and an unsubsidised tariff for everyone else. The figures below are the closest currently available — see the note about changes at the end.

Subsidised residential tariff

Monthly block (kWh)Rate
1 – 30050 fils (0.050 JD) / kWh
301 – 600100 fils (0.100 JD) / kWh
Above 600200 fils (0.200 JD) / kWh

A fixed support discount is deducted automatically: about 2.5 JD/month for usage of 51–200 kWh, and 2.0 JD/month for 201–600 kWh. Above 600 kWh the fixed support is not paid.

Unsubsidised residential tariff

Monthly block (kWh)Rate
1 – 1000120 fils (0.120 JD) / kWh
Above 1000150 fils (0.150 JD) / kWh

Other sectors for reference: commercial ~120 fils/kWh (first 2000 kWh) then 152 fils; small industrial ~60 fils/kWh (first 10,000 kWh); home EV charging ~108–160 fils/kWh depending on off-peak/peak. The total bill also adds the usual fixed regulatory charges (cleaning, TV and lump-sum fees).

💡 Why the block tariff makes solar so attractive here

Because the highest-priced units are the ones above 600 kWh (200 fils) or above 1000 kWh (150 fils), solar is most valuable when it removes those top, most expensive units first. A household pushed into the 200-fils block by air conditioning in summer saves far more per kWh than the headline rate suggests. Estimate your own saving with the solar bill savings calculator.

🔁 Net metering (the game-changer)

Jordan's distribution companies — JEPCO (central), IDECO (north) and EDCO (south/other) — operate net metering and wheeling. Surplus energy your panels export during the day is credited against what you draw at night, so you effectively "bank" summer and midday production. This is the main reason rooftop solar pays back quickly in Jordan. The exact rules and caps change over time, so confirm the current terms with your distribution company before sizing an export system.

🛠️ Field note from Jordan

Across many projects in Jordan — big and small, in several governorates — the tariff changed with the location and its type: residential, commercial, industrial and others. In every case we designed the system to cover the whole bill, so it comes down to 0 JD no matter how the site is classified.

❓ Frequently asked questions

How many peak sun hours does Jordan get?

About 5.5–6 on average; ~6–6.5 in the southern desert (Ma'an, Aqaba) and ~5–5.5 in the north (Irbid, Amman).

Is solar worth it in Jordan?

For most homes yes — high sun hours plus net metering give a typical payback of about 4–7 years.

Does Jordan allow net metering?

Yes, through JEPCO, IDECO and EDCO. Confirm the current terms with your distribution company.

How much does a solar system cost in Jordan?

Roughly 500–800 JD per kW as a guide, so about 2,500–4,000 JD for a 5 kW home system. Get local quotes.

Note: tariffs, net-metering rules and prices in Jordan change over time. The figures here are approximate, for planning only — always verify with your distribution company (JEPCO / IDECO / EDCO) and local installers.
