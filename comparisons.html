Solar comparisons, side by side

The choices that trip people up the most — which charge controller, which battery, which system type, which panel — laid out in clear tables so you can see the trade-offs at a glance.

MPPT vs PWM charge controllers

Both protect your battery; the difference is how much solar energy they capture and what array voltage they can handle.

FeatureMPPTPWM
How it worksDC-to-DC converter; runs the panel at its best voltageConnects the panel almost directly to the battery
Energy capturedHighest — about 15–30% moreLower; wastes panel voltage above the battery
Best array voltageArray voltage much higher than the batteryPanel voltage close to the battery
Cold-weather gainLarge (cold means higher voltage to convert)Little
CostHigherLower
Best forLarger systems, series arrays, off-gridSmall, simple 12 V systems on a budget
Bottom line: choose MPPT for anything above a few hundred watts, series-wired arrays, cold climates, or off-grid; PWM only for small, cheap 12 V setups where panel and battery voltage already match.

Lithium vs lead-acid batteries

Lead-acid is cheaper to buy; lithium is usually cheaper per usable kilowatt-hour over its life. Here's why.

FeatureLithium (LiFePO4)Lead-acid
Usable depth of discharge80–90%~50%
Cycle life3,000–6,000+ cycles500–1,200 cycles
Round-trip efficiency~95–98%~80–85%
Upfront costHighLow
Cost per usable kWh over lifeLowerHigher
Weight & sizeLight and compactHeavy and bulky
MaintenanceNoneWatering (flooded types)
Best forDaily cycling, off-grid, solar storageOccasional backup, tight budgets
Bottom line: for a battery you cycle every day, lithium almost always wins on lifetime cost and usable capacity. Lead-acid still makes sense for cheap, rarely-used backup.

Grid-tied vs off-grid vs hybrid

The system type decides whether you have a battery, whether you keep power in an outage, and how much it costs.

FeatureGrid-tiedOff-gridHybrid
BatteryNoneRequiredYes
Grid connectionYesNoneYes
Power during outageNo (shuts off)Yes (sole source)Yes (backup)
Upfront costLowestHighestHigh
ComplexitySimpleComplexModerate
Best forLowest bills where the grid is reliableSites with no grid accessBackup power plus bill savings
Bottom line: grid-tied for the cheapest savings on a reliable grid, off-grid when there's no grid at all, hybrid when you want savings and blackout protection.

Monocrystalline vs polycrystalline vs thin-film panels

The three main panel technologies trade efficiency, cost, and how much space they need.

FeatureMonocrystallinePolycrystallineThin-film
EfficiencyHighest (~19–22%)Medium (~15–17%)Lowest (~10–13%)
CostHigherMediumLow per watt
Roof space neededLeastMoreMost
Heat & low-lightGood in heatAverageBest in heat and partial shade
AppearanceBlack, uniformBlue, speckledThin, often flexible
Best forLimited roof spaceBudget builds with roomLarge areas, curved or portable surfaces
Bottom line: monocrystalline is the default choice for most rooftops today; polycrystalline saves money when you have space; thin-film suits large or unusual surfaces and hot climates.
