Solar panels in New Zealand

New Zealand's electricity is already mostly renewable — yet rooftop solar is growing fast there, because retail prices are high and self-consumption cuts the bill directly. This guide covers sun hours, the retail market, export rules, and realistic costs and payback for solar in New Zealand.

☀️ Peak sun hours in New Zealand by region

Peak sun hours (PSH) is the number of hours per day the sun delivers 1000 W/m². New Zealand averages about 4.0–4.8 PSH — the sunny north (Auckland, Northland, Bay of Plenty) is highest, and the cooler south (Dunedin, Invercargill) a little lower. Seasons matter: summer days are long and productive; winter output is modest, which is why systems there are sized for the shoulder months too. See our guide on peak sun hours by location.

⚡ Electricity prices and solar rules in New Zealand

NZ retail electricity runs roughly NZ$0.28–0.36/kWh all-in — high by international standards — and there is no national net metering or feed-in tariff. Exported solar is typically bought by your retailer at only ~NZ$0.08–0.10/kWh, so the system economics are pure self-consumption: every kWh you use yourself saves NZ$0.30+, while every exported kWh returns under a third of that. Batteries are common for exactly this reason, and many retailers now offer time-of-use plans where the battery's evening discharge is worth even more. Estimate your saving with the solar bill savings calculator.

💡 Why solar still pays back fast in NZ

High retail prices do the work. A 5 kW system in Auckland produces roughly 5,500–6,300 kWh a year — a large share of a typical home's 9,000 kWh — and at NZ$0.30+/kWh avoided, the saving is real even with near-zero export value. Payback commonly lands around 6–10 years. Since NZ's grid is already ~85% renewable, rooftop solar there is primarily a bill-cutting investment rather than an emissions one — both are good, but the money logic is what matters for your wallet. 🛠️ Field note

The NZ lesson is about sizing for self-consumption. With export worth only ~NZ$0.08–0.10/kWh, a system that is too big simply wastes production. The right approach: profile the household's daytime usage (fridge, heat pump, pool pump, EV charging), size the array to match that curve, and add a battery only if the evening load justifies it. On time-of-use plans, the battery earns its money by discharging during the expensive 5–9 PM window — that is where the NZ maths really works.

❓ Frequently asked questions

Is solar worth it in New Zealand without net metering?

Yes — retail prices of NZ$0.28–0.36/kWh make self-consumption solar pay back in roughly 6–10 years even though export is bought at only ~NZ$0.08–0.10/kWh.

How many sun hours does New Zealand get?

About 4.0–4.8 PSH — Auckland and Northland are highest (~4.6–4.8), the far south lower (~3.8–4.1).

Do I need a battery for solar in NZ?

Not always — but with low export value, a battery meaningfully improves the maths if your evening load (heating, hot water, EV) is significant, especially on time-of-use plans.

How much does a 5kW system cost in New Zealand?

Roughly NZ$10,000–13,500 installed. Prices vary by region and roof — get at least three local quotes.

Note: tariffs, net-billing rules and prices in New Zealand change over time. The figures here are approximate, for planning only — always verify with your utility and local installers.
